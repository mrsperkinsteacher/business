How to Study

Re-reading your notes feels productive, but research shows it is one of the weakest ways to revise. These methods work better.

Close the book and write what you remember

This is active recall, and it is the strongest revision method there is. Read a dot point, close your notes, write or say everything you remember, then check and fill the gaps. It feels harder than re-reading. That is why it works.

Retrieval practice - The Learning Scientists

Try: the Syllabus Skeleton. Fill in the dot points from memory, then check against the syllabus.

Space it out, do not cram

Come back to a topic today, again in a few days, then again next week. Each return makes the memory stronger. Three short sessions beat one long one the night before.

Spaced practice - The Learning Scientists

Try: put a dot point you learnt this week on your calendar for 3 days and 10 days from now.

Use flashcards properly

Say the answer out loud before you flip. If you get it right, it goes to the 'know it' pile. If you get it wrong, it goes back in the pile for tomorrow. Spend your time on the cards you do not know.

Try: the online Operations flashcards on the website.

Learn the verb, then answer the verb

Every HSC question starts with a verb from the NESA glossary. Identify wants a name. Explain wants cause and effect. Evaluate wants a judgement. Underline the verb before you write a word.

Try: your Command Terms Ladder card. Keep it in front of you when you do practice questions.

Know your case studies with dates and figures

Markers reward specific, dated evidence. 'In 2023 the business made a record profit of...' beats 'the business did well'. Build a fact page for each case study business and add one dated fact each week.

Try: divider 7 in your folder.

Explain it to someone else

Explain a dot point out loud to a family member, a friend, or your dog. If you cannot explain it simply, you do not know it yet. The gaps show up fast.

Elaboration - The Learning Scientists

Try: explain one Operations strategy to someone at home tonight in under a minute.

Practise under exam conditions

Do past HSC questions with a timer, no notes and no phone. Then mark it against the marking guidelines. It is the closest thing to the real exam, and the more you do it, the less scary the real day is.

NESA past HSC papers and marking guidelines

Try: about 1.8 minutes per mark. A 20 mark extended response is about 35 minutes.

25 minutes on, 5 minutes off

Set a timer for 25 minutes and do one thing only. When it goes off, take a real 5 minute break: stand up, move, look away from the screen. Then go again. After four rounds, take a longer break.

The Pomodoro Technique

Try: phone in another room for the 25 minutes.

For the Whole of Life: Wisdom

"For the Lord gives wisdom; from his mouth come knowledge and understanding." Proverbs 2:6 (NIV)

Study is more than collecting marks. Ask God for wisdom as you learn, and use what you learn to serve other people well.
