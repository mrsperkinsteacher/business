Command verb cards

open a card

IdentifysurfaceUse when: you just need to name something.
NESA definition

Recognise and name.

What examiners look for

One correct term or name. No explanation needed - adding one doesn’t earn extra marks, but it wastes your time. Keep it short.

Shape your answer

Name it. One term or fact. Move on.

Business example

Identify the legal structure of the business described in the stimulus. (1 mark)

DefinesurfaceUse when: you need to state what something means.
NESA definition

State meaning and identify essential qualities.

What examiners look for

A complete definition that captures the essential features. Do not pad with examples unless the question asks for them. One clear sentence is usually enough.

Shape your answer

Term + what it means + its essential quality, in one clear sentence.

Business example

Define the term ‘goodwill’. (1 mark)

CalculatesurfaceUse when: you need to work out a figure from the information given.
NESA definition

Ascertain/determine from given facts, figures or information.

What examiners look for

Show your working: write the formula, put the numbers in, then give the answer with the right units (dollars, units, per cent). A correct answer with no working can lose marks if there is a slip along the way.

Shape your answer

Formula -> numbers in -> answer with units.

Business example

Calculate the break-even point in units using the information in the table. (2 marks)

OutlinemoderateUse when: you need to cover the main points without going deep.
NESA definition

Sketch in general terms; indicate the main features of.

What examiners look for

Broad coverage, not deep explanation. Think “wide not tall.” Name and briefly state each key feature. You do not need to explain why - save that for Explain questions.

Shape your answer

Main feature 1, main feature 2, main feature 3 - one line each.

Business example

Outline the features of a sole trader as a legal structure. (3 marks)

DescribemoderateUse when: you need to paint a full picture of what something is or looks like.
NESA definition

Provide characteristics and features.

What examiners look for

More detail per feature than an outline, but still not a full cause-and-effect explanation. Think: what does this look like in practice? What are its characteristics?

Shape your answer

Feature + what it looks like in practice, for each feature.

Business example

Describe the challenges a business is likely to face during the maturity stage of the business life cycle. (4 marks)

DistinguishmoderateUse when: two things need to be directly compared by their differences.
NESA definition

Recognise or note/indicate as being distinct or different from; to note differences between.

What examiners look for

Direct contrast. Use language like “whereas,” “unlike,” and “in contrast.” Do not define each term separately - that is not distinguishing. The comparison must be explicit.

Shape your answer

A is ... whereas B is ... (repeat for each difference).

Business example

Distinguish between voluntary and involuntary cessation of a business. (3 marks)

ComparemoderateUse when: you need to show how two things are alike and how they are different.
NESA definition

Show how things are similar or different.

What examiners look for

Cover similarities AND differences, point by point - not one block on each. Words like "both", "similarly", "whereas" and "however" show the examiner you are comparing.

Shape your answer

Both A and B ... / A ... whereas B ... (similarities and differences).

Business example

Compare a sole trader and a partnership as legal structures. (4 marks)

ExaminemoderateUse when: you need to look into an issue closely.
NESA definition

Inquire into.

What examiners look for

Go further than describing. Look at the key features of the issue, what causes it and what it leads to for the business. Use the stimulus or a real business to ground each point.

Shape your answer

Feature -> cause -> effect on the business, with an example.

Business example

Examine the influence of technology on the establishment of a small business. (5 marks)

ExplaindeepUse when: you need to show why or how something happens.
NESA definition

Relate cause and effect; make relationships between things evident; provide why and/or how.

What examiners look for

The word “because” should appear (or be clearly implied). Show the link between a cause and its effect. A common mistake is to describe instead of explain - always go one step further and say why or how.

Shape your answer

Point -> because -> so / therefore -> business example.

Business example

Explain how an increase in interest rates could affect a small business that has taken out a variable-rate bank loan. (4 marks)

AnalysedeepUse when: you need to break something into parts and show how they connect.
NESA definition

Identify components and the relationship between them; draw out and relate implications.

What examiners look for

Name the parts, show how they affect each other, then say what it means for the business - the implications. "This leads to...", "As a result..." and "This means the business..." keep you analysing instead of listing.

Shape your answer

Parts -> how they connect -> implications for the business.

Business example

Analyse the impact of an economic downturn on the stakeholders of a large retailer. (6 marks)

JustifydeepUse when: you need to defend a conclusion with reasons.
NESA definition

Support an argument or conclusion.

What examiners look for

State your position clearly, then back it up with reasoning or evidence. “Because” + “as a result” + “therefore” are useful connectors. You are not asked to consider the other side - just support your conclusion.

Shape your answer

Position -> reason -> evidence -> therefore.

Business example

Justify why a business owner with limited industry experience might choose to buy a franchise rather than start a new business. (3 marks)

RecommenddeepUse when: you need to choose a course of action and back it up.
NESA definition

Provide reasons in favour.

What examiners look for

Make a clear recommendation early, then give the reasons it suits THIS business - link every reason to the scenario. If there are options, say why yours is better than the others.

Shape your answer

My recommendation is ... because ... (linked to the scenario).

Business example

Recommend a suitable source of finance for the business owner in the stimulus. (3 marks)

DiscussdeepUse when: you need to explore both sides of an argument.
NESA definition

Identify issues and provide points for and/or against.

What examiners look for

Both sides of the argument. Acknowledge complexity. Come to a reasoned conclusion. One-sided responses lose marks at the higher mark levels. Do not just list points - connect them and reach a position.

Shape your answer

For ... / Against ... / On balance ...

Business example

“The maturity stage is the most dangerous stage of the business life cycle.” Discuss this statement with reference to the stimulus. (6 marks)

AssessdeepUse when: you need to weigh something up and make a judgement.
NESA definition

Make a judgement of value, quality, outcomes, results or size.

What examiners look for

Do not just describe - you must arrive at a judgement. Weigh the evidence and state how significant, effective or important something is. Your conclusion must be supported by the points you have made.

Shape your answer

Points weighed up -> how significant -> clear judgement.

Business example

Assess the importance of a well-developed business plan to the long-term success of an SME. (6 marks)

EvaluatedeepUse when: you need to judge something against clear criteria.
NESA definition

Make a judgement based on criteria; determine the value of.

What examiners look for

Similar to assess, but implies you are measuring against stated criteria. Set up what “success” or “effectiveness” looks like, then judge the evidence against it. A strong conclusion is essential - do not leave the reader uncertain of your position.

Shape your answer

Criteria -> evidence against the criteria -> judgement.

Business example

Evaluate the effectiveness of the corrective actions taken by a business in response to falling sales performance. (6 marks)

NESA definitions are from the NESA HSC Glossary of key words. "What examiners look for", "Shape your answer" and the Business examples are Mrs Perkins' guidance and practice questions, not past HSC questions.
