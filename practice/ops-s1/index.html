2. Discuss the balance between cost and quality in operations strategy. (6 marks)

Write your answer in your book first. Then open the marking guide and mark it yourself.

Show the marking guide
MarksWhat it looks like
5-6Thorough, covers several aspects, uses an actual business, makes a judgement where needed
3-4Sound, covers some aspects, limited judgement or example
1-2Some relevant information

Answers could include:

  • Higher quality usually needs better inputs, training and checks, which raise costs
  • Lower costs can mean lower quality and more returns
  • Poor quality also has costs (waste, warranty claims, lost reputation)
  • Balance depends on strategy: cost leadership vs differentiation
  • Points for and against, with an actual example

C  Mini Business Report

Recommend  |  Outcomes H8, H9

Little Feet Shoes makes children's school shoes in Sydney. Parents complain that soles wear out in three months. Fixing this would raise costs by 15%, and cheaper imported shoes already undercut its prices.

Task: Recommend how Little Feet Shoes should balance cost and quality.

About 250 words, written as a business report. Headings: Introduction / Current situation / Recommendations / Conclusion.

Check your report: a high-level response would include
  • Identifies the cost-quality trade-off
  • Recommends improving quality (better soles) and differentiating on durability
  • Supports with quality guarantee or warranty and marketing to parents
  • Suggests ways to offset costs (supplier negotiation, waste reduction)

D  Case Study Collector

Analyse  |  Outcome H7

Business: Zara (Inditex)

Research and add to your Operations Case Study Bank:

  • Which performance objectives Zara focuses on
  • One fact about its speed to market, with source and date
  • How its operations achieve this
  • What it gives up (the trade-off)

Use a reliable source from the last 3 years where you can. Record the source name and date.

Check your entry: what to look for
  • Speed and flexibility as the main objectives
  • Dated evidence (e.g. weeks from design to store)
  • Clear trade-off identified
